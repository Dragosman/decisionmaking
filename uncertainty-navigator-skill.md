# Uncertainty Navigator

A facilitation skill for navigating uncertainty using Pfleiderer's pre-mortem and
pre-celebration methods, layered with subtle bias detection through indirect questioning.

---

## Core Concepts

**Pre-Mortem**: Assume the favored alternative has *completely failed*. Use prospective hindsight
to look back from a future failure and explain why. Combats overconfidence and confirmation bias.

**Pre-Celebration**: Assume a *less-favored alternative* turned out to be a huge success.
Look back and explain why. Reveals unsuspected opportunities and overlooked factors.

**Temporal Flexibility**: The pre-mortem is not only a pre-decision tool. It can be applied
*mid-project or post-decision* (e.g., 6 months or 1 year in) to stress-test the current trajectory,
surface emerging risks, and trigger course corrections before failure actually happens.

**Bias Fishing**: The most dangerous biases don't announce themselves. Ask indirect questions
to surface them, then reflect them back as "patterns" or "tendencies" rather than naming them
as biases until the evidence is clear and it's useful for the user to hear the label.

---

## Session Flow

### Step 0 — Orient the Session

#### 0a — Respect Explicit Intent First

If the user has explicitly named the tool they want, use it immediately. No triage needed.

| Explicit signal | Action |
|---|---|
| "run a premortem", "premortem this", "what could go wrong" | Go straight to Step 2 (Pre-Mortem) |
| "pre-celebration", "what if the other option succeeded", "what am I missing in the alternatives" | Go straight to Step 3 (Pre-Celebration) |

Only proceed to triage (0b) if the user hasn't signaled a preference.

---

#### 0b — Behavioral Triage (when no explicit tool is named)

Do not ask the user which tool they need — they likely don't know, and asking directly
invites rationalization rather than honest self-assessment.

Instead, ask one open question and **read the language they use in response**:

> *"Tell me about the situation and where your head is at right now."*

Then listen for behavioral signals:

**Deploy Pre-Mortem if you observe:**
- Heavy investment in describing the favored option (detail, energy, certainty)
- Alternatives are barely mentioned or only appear when prompted
- Risks are framed as "unlikely", "handled", or glossed over
- The user speaks about the favored path as essentially decided
- Confidence feels high relative to the complexity of the situation

**Deploy Pre-Celebration if you observe:**
- The user freely acknowledges the favored option's risks but dismisses alternatives quickly
- Non-favored options are described with negative framing before being examined
- The user seems open to uncertainty but hasn't genuinely explored what the other path could offer
- There's tunnel vision on execution of the current choice, not on whether it's the right choice

**Hold both in reserve if:**
- The user seems genuinely uncertain, open framing, no strong pull toward one option
- In this case, run Pre-Mortem first; introduce Pre-Celebration only if the pre-mortem surfaces
  no meaningful risks (which itself may signal over-attachment worth probing from the other direction)

**Never run both sequentially as a default.** One well-run exercise beats two rushed ones.

---

### Step 1 — Bias Fishing (Indirect)

Before starting the structured exercises, ask 3–5 probing questions designed to surface
cognitive patterns without naming them. Listen for:

**Overconfidence markers**:
- "How confident are you that this will work, on a scale of 1–10?"
- "Have you seen this kind of thing succeed before? What makes this situation similar?"
- "If I told you there's a 30% chance this fails, how would that land?"

**Confirmation bias markers**:
- "Who have you talked to about this? Did they mostly agree or push back?"
- "What's the strongest argument *against* your current plan?"
- "Have you actively looked for evidence that this won't work?"

**Status quo / sunk cost markers**:
- "How long have you been working on this direction?"
- "If you were starting fresh today with no prior commitments, would you make the same call?"
- "What would it take to change your mind at this point?"

**Group think / social pressure markers**:
- "Is the team aligned on this? Was there any dissent?"
- "Would anyone on your team feel uncomfortable saying this is a bad idea?"

Do NOT name these as biases yet. Take mental note of patterns. Return to them at Step 4.

---

### Step 2 — Pre-Mortem

**Framing (say something like this)**:
> "Let's fast-forward. It's [12 months / 2 years / end of project] from now. Your chosen path
> — [restate it] — has completely fallen apart. Not just underperformed. Failed. The postmortem
> is happening and everyone's trying to understand what went wrong.
> What happened?"

**Facilitation moves**:
1. Start broad: *"What's the single most likely cause of failure?"*
2. Push for specifics: *"What does that actually look like in practice? Who notices first? What breaks first?"*
3. Go systemic: *"What was the assumption that turned out to be wrong?"*
4. Go human: *"Was there a moment someone could have flagged this but didn't? Why?"*
5. Go external: *"What did the environment do that you didn't anticipate?"*

**Temporal pre-mortem (post-decision / mid-project variant)**:
If the user has already committed to a path, shift the frame:
> "It's [X months] from now. You're doing an honest review. The project is in serious trouble —
> not catastrophic yet, but clearly off-track. What are the top 3 reasons that explains it?"

Track the failure modes that surface. Prioritize by:
- Probability: *"How likely is this, realistically?"*
- Impact: *"If this happened, how bad would it be?"*
- Controllability: *"Is this something you could have caught earlier or mitigated?"*

---

### Step 3 — Pre-Celebration

**Framing (say something like this)**:
> "Now let's flip it. Imagine you'd gone with [the less-favored or non-chosen option] instead.
> It's the same future date — and that decision turned out to be a massive, unexpected win.
> People are talking about it as a case study in getting it right.
> Why did it work so well?"

**Facilitation moves**:
1. *"What specifically did that alternative enable that your current plan doesn't?"*
2. *"What was the hidden upside that wasn't obvious at the time?"*
3. *"Who benefited most, and why?"*
4. *"What was the environment like that made this alternative shine?"*
5. *"Did this path avoid a risk that your current plan runs into?"*

The goal is not to make the user switch — it's to surface factors they may have discounted
or overlooked when they locked in their preference.

---

### Step 4 — Bias Reflection

After both exercises, reflect back any patterns you noticed during Step 1 and the exercises
themselves. Use a non-judgmental, observational frame:

**Before naming a bias**:
> "I noticed something in how you talked about this — you seemed very certain about X
> even before we started, and when I pushed on Y you moved past it quickly. Does that
> feel accurate to you?"

**After confirmation**:
> "That pattern has a name — it's called [X bias]. It's extremely common in high-stakes
> decisions and it's not a character flaw, it's how the brain manages uncertainty. Here's why
> it matters in your specific situation..."

**Common biases to watch for and how they show up in this context**:

| Pattern | How it manifests | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| Overconfidence | High certainty scores, dismissing failure scenarios quickly | "You rated confidence at 8/10 early on, but we found 4 failure modes in 10 minutes. Worth recalibrating?" |
| Confirmation bias | Only cited supporting evidence, difficulty arguing against | "You mentioned 3 reasons this will work but struggled to find strong counterarguments — that gap is worth looking at." |
| Sunk cost | Resistance to alternatives because of time/money already spent | "You mentioned you've been on this path for 18 months a few times. Would a fresh set of eyes see this differently?" |
| Groupthink | Team alignment cited as validation, no dissent surfaced | "Perfect consensus can sometimes mean concerns are going unspoken rather than not existing." |
| Planning fallacy | Failure scenarios all felt like edge cases | "Most of the risks we surfaced were called 'unlikely' — but collectively they add up to significant exposure." |

---

### Step 5 — Synthesis & Action

Close the session with a synthesis calibrated to which tool was used:

**If Pre-Mortem was run:**
1. **Top 3 failure modes** — ranked by probability × impact
2. **1 assumption to validate** — the single most dangerous unverified belief
3. **1 mitigation move** — most actionable risk reduction step available now
4. **Bias check** — one pattern observed during the session worth watching

**If Pre-Celebration was run:**
1. **Top 2 overlooked upsides** of the non-favored alternative — factors to incorporate or monitor
2. **1 question to reopen** — the aspect of the favored option most worth reconsidering
3. **Bias check** — one pattern observed during the session worth watching

**If both were run:**
1. Top 3 risks (pre-mortem) vs. Top 2 upsides of alternative (pre-celebration)
2. 1 assumption to validate
3. 1 mitigation move
4. Bias check

Ask: *"Is there anything we surfaced today that changes how you'd approach the next 30 days?"*

---

## Facilitation Principles

- **Never rush to the risk list.** The quality of a pre-mortem scales with how vividly the user
  can imagine the failure. Take time to make the failure scenario feel real.
- **Don't let the user escape with "unlikely."** Every scenario gets pushed: *"What would have
  to be true for this to happen?"*
- **Protect the pre-celebration.** Users often resist imagining success for alternatives they've
  rejected. Hold the frame.
- **Name biases late.** The goal of bias fishing is insight, not confrontation. If a user gets
  defensive about being told they're overconfident, you've lost the session. Build the case first.
- **Prospective hindsight is the engine.** Keep returning the user to *"You're in the future,
  looking back."* This activates different thinking than *"What might go wrong?"*

---

## Temporal Variants

| Timing | Frame | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-decision | Classic pre-mortem / pre-celebration | Choose better |
| 30–90 days post-launch | Early warning pre-mortem | Catch early signals |
| 6–12 months in | Mid-project trajectory check | Course correct |
| Post-project | Retrospective pre-mortem | Extract learnings |

For mid-project sessions, add: *"What are you currently ignoring or postponing that might
be the leading indicator of the failure scenario we just described?"*
